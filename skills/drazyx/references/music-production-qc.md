# Music Production, Mix Review, and Audio QC

Use this reference when producing music, discussing arrangements or sound design, reviewing a mix, or determining whether an exported master is technically ready.

## Separate the task type

First classify the request:
- **Production / composition:** writing, arrangement, sound selection, sound design, musical identity, dynamics, and emotional development.
- **Mix review:** balance, masking, frequency relationships, dynamics, stereo, depth, transient control, and translation.
- **Master QC:** inspect the final delivered file for technical and perceptual defects.
- **Mastering:** intentional processing to achieve a requested sonic and delivery target.

Do not silently turn QC into mastering. If the user asks for an audit, report issues and corrective recommendations; do not process or replace the master unless explicitly asked.

## Production feedback

Review the track as music, not merely as a technical file:
- Is the central motif memorable and recognizable?
- Does the arrangement develop, create contrast, and maintain attention?
- Are transitions earned by the harmony, rhythm, melody, texture, or energy?
- Do drums and bass support the groove without masking the musical identity?
- Do sound choices reinforce the track's mood and genre?
- Are repeated sections varied enough to remain engaging?
- Does the intro establish the world quickly, and does the ending feel intentional?

Give prioritized recommendations. Distinguish essential structural improvements from optional creative alternatives.

## Mix review

Evaluate:
- **Low end:** kick/bass separation, sub stability, note consistency, rumble, masking, and mono compatibility.
- **Midrange:** vocal or lead intelligibility, melodic body, harshness, congestion, and masking.
- **High end:** presence, air, sibilance, brittle transients, and fatigue.
- **Dynamics:** transient integrity, pumping, over-compression, uncontrolled peaks, and contrast between sections.
- **Stereo:** width by frequency range, phase coherence, mono fold-down, and localization.
- **Depth:** front/back hierarchy, reverb and delay balance, pre-delay, decay, masking, and intelligibility.
- **Translation:** headphones, phone speaker, small speakers, car, and mono playback where available.
- **Noise and artifacts:** clicks, pops, clipping, distortion, unwanted hiss, edits, and tails.

Use listening and objective measurements together. State which problems are audible, which are measured, and which are only possible risks.

## Master QC

Inspect the exact final file intended for release or licensing. Check:
- file integrity, codec, sample rate, bit depth, channels, duration, and metadata;
- sample peak and true peak when a suitable meter is available;
- integrated loudness and loudness range when relevant to the delivery target;
- clipping, inter-sample peak risk, distortion, unwanted limiting, or codec damage;
- silence, clipped fades, clicks, abrupt edits, and missing tails;
- phase, stereo integrity, and mono compatibility;
- consistency between the approved master, distribution file, and any preview version.

Do not invent a universal loudness target. Confirm the platform, distributor, sync/licensing specification, or client delivery requirement and distinguish a technical ceiling from a desired creative loudness.

## Reference comparison

- Use references that share the relevant genre, energy, arrangement, or sonic goal.
- Match playback loudness as closely as practical before judging tonal balance or impact.
- Compare specific dimensions: low-end weight, transient shape, vocal/lead placement, width, depth, brightness, and dynamics.
- Do not copy a reference's exact master level or assume its processing chain is appropriate.
- Explain what is transferable and what is specific to the reference track.

## Tools and environment

The artist commonly works in FL Studio 24 with Serum and a large plugin collection, including FabFilter Pro-Q and Pro-C tools. Treat these as available preferences, not mandatory dependencies:
- inspect the actual project and plugin availability before recommending a specific chain;
- explain the purpose of each suggested process;
- avoid recommending multiple plugins that perform the same job without a reason;
- avoid preset dumping and arbitrary parameter values;
- give a starting range only when supported by the source and the intended effect, then require listening and gain-matched comparison.

When the project or audio cannot be auditioned, say so. Signal analysis can reveal timing, peaks, spectral energy, and possible defects, but cannot prove that a mix feels balanced, emotional, wide, or professional.
