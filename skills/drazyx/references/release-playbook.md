# Release and Distribution Playbook

Use this reference for single, EP, album, instrumental, beat, or remix campaign preparation.

## 1. Confirm release facts

Before writing public copy or preparing artwork, verify:
- exact track/release title and artist spelling;
- release or album name;
- version (original, instrumental, slowed, remix, clean, explicit, etc.);
- credits, collaborators, writers, producers, and rights ownership;
- release date and time zone;
- distribution status and available platforms;
- correct cover artwork and destination URLs;
- any licensing, sample, interpolation, fan-work, or permission constraints.

Do not fill missing metadata with assumptions. Keep unknown fields blank or ask the artist.

## 2. Pre-release

Build anticipation with a specific reason to care:
- identify the strongest musical moment and the audience it may connect with;
- establish one clear visual/messaging direction;
- prepare a short sequence of distinct assets: discovery hook, production/process asset, release context, and a date announcement when appropriate;
- prepare verified artwork, metadata, platform links, and profile assets;
- confirm that all public claims and credits are accurate.

Do not over-rely on pre-save campaigns. Use them when the distributor and release setup support them and when there is a real plan to convert the audience.

## 3. Release period

- Make the full-track destination easy to find.
- Use one clear release announcement, then support it with different musical or narrative angles rather than reposting the same announcement.
- Coordinate short-form content, artist profile, streaming links, and YouTube/SoundCloud/Bandcamp destinations where relevant.
- Verify the track is actually live on the platforms named in copy before saying it is available there.
- Keep the release artwork, title, credits, and version consistent across assets.

## 4. Post-release

A release does not stop being useful after release day:
- introduce a different section, arrangement detail, lyric, sound-design detail, or listener interpretation;
- respond to real comments and questions;
- revisit content that generated meaningful saves, shares, retention, or profile visits;
- make new versions only when they offer a real new experience;
- document performance and lessons for the next release.

Avoid reposting identical assets repeatedly without a new angle.

## 5. Distribution and account safety

- Follow the current distributor's metadata, artwork, audio, rights, and delivery rules.
- Confirm that the artist has the rights or permissions needed for samples, remixes, cover art, fan content, and promotional footage.
- Never buy streams, use bots, manipulate playlist systems, or pay for guaranteed placement.
- Never promise playlist editorial support, algorithmic placement, or monetization outcomes.
- Keep master WAVs, instrumental versions, artwork masters, credits, and final promo exports organized and backed up.

## 6. Campaign closeout

After the campaign window, summarize:
- assets published and their destinations;
- performance by platform and post;
- the strongest audience response and the evidence behind it;
- what failed or underperformed;
- what should be repeated, changed, or stopped;
- the next concrete experiment.

A campaign review must recommend an action. Do not present vanity metrics without interpretation.
