# Drazyx — Artist and Brand Context

This reference consolidates the artist identity already documented in the official Drazyx repository and the artist's established creative preferences. Treat it as a working identity system, not a rigid preset.

## Artist identity

Drazyx is an independent Brazilian music producer and artist. The project builds small worlds from late nights, nostalgia, emotion, and internet culture. The music spans Jersey Club, Hip Hop, Trap, Electronic, Lo-fi, Phonk, and adjacent melodic or atmospheric directions.

The sound can move between:
- emotionally led melodies and memorable motifs;
- atmospheric textures, pads, synths, pianos, and spacious reverbs/delays;
- deep, controlled low end;
- rhythmic impact and movement, including club-derived patterns;
- melancholic, intimate, nocturnal, nostalgic, or energetic moods, depending on the track.

Do not reduce every release to the same mood. Identify the actual track's genre, energy, emotional center, and audience before deciding its visual or marketing direction.

**Core principle:** music first, business second. The commercial layer should help the music find its audience without making the artist feel artificial.

## Visual identity

The recurring world is dark, intimate, nocturnal, minimal, and internet-native: a digital bedroom with lo-fi texture, pixel/retro-futurist details, and editorial composition.

### Color system

| Role | Color |
|---|---|
| Deep background | `#070810` |
| Secondary dark | `#11101A` |
| Warm dark surface | `#18131D` |
| Primary text | `#F2EDF5` |
| Secondary text | `#A99EAE` |
| Accent / light source | `#B84DFF` |

Use purple as a precise light source, highlight, or accent—not as a large flat fill or constant neon glow. Keep contrast deliberate and preserve readability on small screens.

### Typography

- **Space Grotesk:** display, titles, strong short phrases.
- **Inter:** supporting information, metadata, captions, interface-like text.

Prefer restrained hierarchy and readable size. Avoid excessive tracking, too many weights, tiny metadata, and typography that competes with the music.

## Creative characteristics

Aim for:
- atmosphere with clear focal hierarchy;
- controlled negative space and a deliberate frame;
- tactile detail, subtle grain, depth, and restrained lighting;
- visual motion that follows musical phrasing;
- recognizable consistency across releases without forcing every visual into one template.

Avoid by default:
- stock footage that could belong to any artist;
- generic audio visualizers and equalizer bars;
- constant text overlays;
- excessive glow, random glitch, random transitions, or gratuitous camera movement;
- over-designed effects that distract from the track;
- fake luxury, fake scale, or visual claims the artist cannot support.

## Voice and copy personality

Drazyx communication should feel confident, thoughtful, understated, and artist-led. It can be emotional without being melodramatic and promotional without sounding desperate.

- Use concrete language and a distinct point of view.
- Avoid generic motivational copy, empty superlatives, forced slang, and repetitive "out now" language.
- Do not invent a backstory, meaning, feature, credit, collaboration, or personal claim.
- On-screen text is often preferred in English for visual assets, but confirm or infer language from the release and intended audience. Captions and metadata should match the campaign's target market.

## Decision rule

The artist brand is a signature, not a filter. Keep the recurring palette, restraint, and editorial sensibility where they strengthen the work. Let a track's own sonic identity determine the rest.
